# S5: Stale-onset policy

## Question
When does a stale-oracle pause period start, given that nobody may observe the feed while it is stale? An adversary who controls oracle publish times and observation times must not gain discount time beyond the schedule over fresh (unpaused) time, within one block (spec §5.7, ADR-007).

## Policies
- (a) `AtObservation`: the pause starts when someone first observes the invalid feed.
- (b) `DerivedFromOracle`: the pause starts at `last_seen_oracle_ts + max_age`; a stale gap that recovered unseen is closed on the next observation of a newer valid publish.

## Method
Property test `crates/auction_core/tests/s5_pause_onset.rs`: random publish gaps (1..400 ms, up to 12), random extra observation times, random end time, `max_age` 100 ms, one block = 10 ms. Ground truth: fresh time = ms in [start, end) where the latest publish is at most `max_age` old. Property: `t_eff <= fresh + BLOCK` under policy (b), with an observation at every publish and at `end`.

Run: `PROPTEST_CASES=5000 cargo test -p auction_core --test s5_pause_onset` -> 2 passed (5000 cases for the property; the default is 256).

Policy (a) is shown by a deterministic test to overcount: publishes at 0 and 1000, observed only at 1000, end 1100 gives `t_eff` far above fresh time.

## Evaluation-time-observation assumption (ruling)
The first run failed with the shrunk counterexample pubs=[0,64,423,493,880,1239], no extra observations, end=606: `t_eff`=347 vs fresh=336 (excess 11 > 10). The feed went stale at 593 and `end`=606 was never observed, so a 13 ms stale tail was counted. `effective_elapsed` cannot know of it (no oracle_ts/max_age). This was a flaw in the test model, not in `observed`: in the engine every call that evaluates `t_eff` (`liquidate`, `bid`, and the SDK preview simulation) performs the pause observation at the same `now` first (spec §5.7). The property therefore observes at `end` too. With that observation the pause opens at derived onset 493+100=593, 13 ms is subtracted, `t_eff`=334 <= 336.

Consequence: off-chain previews and any other consumer of `effective_elapsed` must simulate the same observation (`observed(...)` at `now`) before evaluating it; reading the stored clock alone can overcount an open unobserved stale tail.

## Residual of policy (b)
It is exact only if every oracle publish is observed (the engine observes on `liquidate`/`bid`; the app and keepers call `observe_feed` on refresh), and a re-stamping TWAP producer (one that republishes old data with fresh timestamps) cannot be detected. Documented in THREAT_MODEL.md.

## Decision
Adopt `DerivedFromOracle`. ADR-007 is Accepted (S5).
